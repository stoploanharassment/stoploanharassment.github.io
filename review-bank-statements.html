Review Your Bank Statements

Understand how your UPI transactions may expose personal contacts to recovery agencies, protect your financial privacy, and report suspected data scraping.

What Your Bank Statements May Reveal

When applying for digital loans, you may have shared your bank statements directly with lenders or authorised access through Account Aggregator consents.

Your bank statement contains much more than your income and expenses. It may also contain the names, mobile numbers, UPI IDs, and transaction details of people with whom you have exchanged money.

Some borrowers have reported that loan recovery agents are allegedly extracting personal contact information from bank statements and UPI transaction descriptions.

This practice, commonly referred to as data scraping, may expose your friends, family members, colleagues, and other individuals to unwanted recovery calls, even if you never provided their details as loan references.

1. Understand How Your Bank Statements May Expose Your Contacts

Depending on your bank and payment application, UPI transaction descriptions may contain:

  • Mobile numbers of payment recipients or senders.
  • UPI IDs containing mobile numbers.
  • Names of friends and family members.
  • Names of colleagues and business contacts.
  • Transaction amounts, dates, and payment frequency.
  • Personal payment descriptions.

Recovery agencies may extract this information and attempt to contact individuals associated with these transactions to put additional pressure on borrowers.

Even if you have never granted a loan application access to your phone contacts, your previously shared bank statements may be another potential source of contact information.

Revoking contact permissions alone may not be enough to protect your personal network.

2. Review Your Last Six Months of Bank Statements

Download the last six months of bank statements from all bank accounts that you have shared with digital lending companies.

Carefully review your UPI transactions and identify individuals whose mobile numbers or other identifiable information appear in the transaction descriptions.

Pay particular attention to transactions involving your parents, siblings, relatives, close friends, colleagues, landlords, and other trusted individuals.

Transaction Type Information Potentially Exposed
UPI Payments to Friends Name, UPI ID or mobile number.
Money Received from Family Sender's name and transaction details.
Payments to Colleagues Name or payment identifier.
Rent Payments Landlord's name or UPI ID.
Repeated Personal Transactions Frequently contacted individuals.
UPI IDs Containing Mobile Numbers Potential direct contact numbers.
Review Previously Shared Statements

Review older statements as well if you previously shared them with lenders. Information in those statements may still be retained by the recipient.

Keep your review private. Do not upload your bank statements or personal contact lists to unverified websites, online tools, or third-party applications.

3. Identify and Inform Potentially Affected Contacts

Once you have reviewed your bank statements, prepare a private list of individuals whose contact information may have been exposed.

Consider informing your close friends, family members, and other potentially affected individuals about the possibility of unwanted calls.

You do not necessarily need to disclose your entire financial situation. You can inform them about suspected misuse of personal information and request that they remain cautious about unknown callers.

Ask them not to disclose your personal information, employment details, residential address, or other contact numbers to unknown individuals.

If they receive threatening or abusive calls, request that they preserve the call logs and screenshots for reporting purposes.

4. Prevent Further Access to Updated Bank Statements

If you have granted recurring Account Aggregator consents, lenders may be able to retrieve updated transaction information within the authorised scope.

Review and revoke unnecessary Account Aggregator consents to prevent further data retrieval under those permissions.

Revoke Account Aggregator Consents

Previously Collected Information May Still Be Retained

Revoking consent does not automatically delete previously collected bank statements.

Lenders may still retain financial information collected earlier, subject to applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

5. Protect Your Privacy During Future Transactions

Review your UPI profile and consider using a UPI ID that does not contain your mobile number, where your payment provider supports this option.

Avoid unnecessarily sharing bank statements with unverified loan applications, loan agents, or third-party intermediaries.

Before sharing any financial document, understand:

  • Why the financial document is required.
  • Who will have access to the information.
  • How long the information will be retained.
  • Whether it will be shared with third-party service providers.
  • Whether recurring access is being requested.
Changing Your UPI ID Has Limitations

Changing your UPI ID does not remove information from previous bank statements or guarantee that future transactions will conceal your mobile number.

6. Report Suspected Data Scraping and Misuse

If recovery agents contact individuals whom you never provided as references, raise a formal complaint with the concerned lender and its grievance department.

Request a written investigation addressing the following questions:

  • How did the recovery agent obtain the individual's mobile number?
  • Was the information extracted from previously submitted bank statements?
  • Was it obtained through Account Aggregator data?
  • Was your financial information shared with external recovery agencies?
  • What authorisation permitted the collection and use of this contact information?
  • What corrective action has been taken to prevent further misuse?

Preserve all available evidence, including screenshots, call logs, WhatsApp messages, emails, and grievance responses.

If the issue remains unresolved, escalate your complaint through the appropriate regulatory and cybercrime reporting channels.

RBI Complaint Management System cms.rbi.org.in

RBI complaints are subject to applicable eligibility requirements. Generally, you should first complain to the regulated entity and escalate if its response is unsatisfactory or no response is received within 30 days.

Use cybercrime reporting channels for suspected cyber offences and Sanchar Saathi's applicable reporting facilities for suspicious communications.

Accessing financial information for an authorised lending purpose does not justify harassment, intimidation, public humiliation, or inappropriate disclosure of a borrower's debt to unrelated individuals.

Your Personal Relationships Are Not Recovery Tools

Your friends, family members, and colleagues should not become targets of harassment simply because they have exchanged money with you through UPI.

A financial transaction between two individuals does not make either person responsible for the other's debts.

Do not let the fear of embarrassment or exposure force you into taking another loan.

Protect your privacy, inform trusted individuals, document inappropriate recovery practices, and work towards resolving your financial situation.

Being in debt does not give anyone unrestricted permission to exploit your personal relationships or misuse your financial information.
Review your transactions. Protect your contacts. Demand accountability.

Disclaimer: The extraction of contact information from bank statements is a potential method of data scraping reported by borrowers. The actual source of contact information must be established through investigation. Not all lenders engage in such practices, and legitimate financial data access should not be confused with unauthorised data misuse.
