Revoke Account Aggregator Consents

Understand who can access your financial information, review active data-sharing permissions, and regain control of your financial privacy.

Who Can Access Your Financial Information?

When applying for digital loans, you may have provided consent through an Account Aggregator (AA) to allow a lender to access your bank account information, transaction history, salary credits, and other financial details.

Many borrowers assume that lenders can access their bank statements only once, during the loan application process. However, depending on the consent granted, lenders may be authorised to retrieve updated financial information periodically, even after the loan has been disbursed.

An active recurring consent may allow a lender to identify when your salary is credited, how much money you receive, which payments you make, your account balance, and other transaction details included within the authorised scope.

Providing consent during a loan application does not mean you must continue sharing your financial information indefinitely.

1. Why Should You Review Your Account Aggregator Consents?

An active consent may allow an authorised lender to access information such as:

  • Salary credit dates and amounts.
  • Bank account balances.
  • Incoming and outgoing transactions.
  • Payments made to other lenders.
  • UPI transactions and available transaction descriptions.
  • Updated bank statements.
  • Other financial information covered under the consent.

The frequency, duration, and scope of this access depend on the consent you originally approved. Not every consent permits recurring access.

If you have taken loans from multiple digital lending platforms, you may have several active Account Aggregator consents without realising it.

2. How Lenders May Monitor Your Financial Activity

There are different purposes for which a lender may request Account Aggregator consent.

Consent Purpose How It May Be Used
Loan Assessment Reviewing your financial position before approving a loan.
Loan Monitoring Periodically retrieving financial information during the loan tenure.
Loan Collection Retrieving authorised financial information when repayments are overdue.

Sahamati's Code of Conduct addresses loan monitoring and collection consents as recognised use cases within the Account Aggregator ecosystem.

How Salary and Transaction Monitoring May Work

For example, if you have authorised recurring access to your salary account, a lender may be able to obtain updated transaction information after your salary is credited.

Depending on the consent scope, the lender may also be able to identify payments you have subsequently made to other lenders, businesses, or individuals through that account.

This does not mean every lender is continuously monitoring your account. Access depends on the authorised purpose, frequency, duration, and scope of the consent.

Reviewing your active consents can help you understand and restrict unnecessary financial data sharing.

3. Review and Revoke Unnecessary Consents

Follow these steps to review your Account Aggregator permissions:

  1. Search your registered email and SMS messages for Account Aggregator notifications.
  2. Identify which Account Aggregators were used during your loan applications.
  3. Visit the respective official Account Aggregator website or mobile application.
  4. Log in using your registered mobile number or existing AA credentials.
  5. Navigate to the Active Consents or Manage Consents section.
  6. Review the lender's name, consent purpose, access frequency, expiry date, and linked bank accounts.
  7. Revoke consents that are no longer necessary, after considering any contractual implications.
  8. Verify that the consent status has changed to Revoked.
  9. Save screenshots and confirmation records for future reference.
Uninstalling a Loan Application Is Not Enough

Uninstalling a loan application does not automatically revoke Account Aggregator consent.

You must separately review and revoke active consents through the respective Account Aggregator platform.

5. Understand Your Right to Revoke Consent

Under the RBI's Account Aggregator framework, customers must be provided with a facility to withdraw previously granted financial data-sharing consent.

Once a consent has been successfully revoked, further financial data retrieval under that particular consent should stop.

However, keep the following points in mind:

  • Revoking consent does not automatically delete financial information already collected by a lender.
  • Revoking consent does not cancel your outstanding loan.
  • Account Aggregators manage financial data sharing and cannot themselves debit money from your bank account in their capacity as an Account Aggregator.
  • Revoking AA consent does not automatically cancel eNACH or UPI AutoPay mandates.
  • You may need to review multiple Account Aggregator platforms if you have taken loans from different lenders.
  • Do not approve new consent requests without understanding their purpose, duration, and frequency.
Account Aggregator Consent and eNACH Mandates Are Different

Account Aggregator consent authorises financial data sharing, whereas an eNACH mandate authorises automatic payment deductions.

Both permissions must be reviewed separately.

Report Suspected Financial Data Misuse

If you suspect that your financial information is being accessed beyond the authorised scope or misused for harassment, raise a written complaint with the Account Aggregator and the concerned lender's grievance department.

Request details of the consent, the authorised purpose, and the available financial data-access history.

Where applicable, escalate unresolved complaints through the RBI Complaint Management System.

Preserve consent records, revocation confirmations, screenshots, and grievance communications as supporting evidence.

Regain Control of Your Financial Privacy

Your bank statement is a sensitive financial document. It may reveal your salary, spending habits, personal transactions, financial commitments, and payments made to other individuals.

Being in debt does not mean you should surrender control over your financial privacy.

Take time to understand the permissions you have granted. Regularly review your active financial data-sharing consents, revoke unnecessary access, and remain cautious whenever a lender requests fresh consent.

Continue working towards a realistic repayment plan while protecting your personal and financial information.

Your repayment obligations and your right to financial privacy are two separate matters.
