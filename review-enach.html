Review Your eNACH Mandates

Understand your automatic payment authorisations, avoid unnecessary charges, and regain control of your finances.

Take Control of Your Bank Account

When you take multiple loans, you may authorise lenders to automatically debit your bank account through eNACH, NACH, UPI AutoPay, or other standing instructions. Over time, you may lose track of how many active mandates are linked to your bank account.

If you are experiencing financial difficulties, multiple automatic debit attempts and failed transactions may result in additional bounce charges, making your financial situation even worse.

Understanding who is authorised to debit your bank account is an important step towards regaining financial control.

1. Identify All Active Mandates

Review all your bank accounts and identify active automatic payment instructions, including:

  • eNACH and NACH mandates.
  • UPI AutoPay mandates.
  • Debit card standing instructions.
  • Recurring payment authorisations.
  • Mandates associated with payday loans and personal loans.

Check your internet banking portal, mobile banking application, registered email, and SMS notifications to identify existing mandates.

2. Request Cancellation of Unnecessary Mandates

If you are unable to maintain your existing repayment schedule due to financial difficulties, review whether cancelling relevant automatic debit mandates is appropriate.

You can request cancellation through:

  • Your bank's internet banking portal or mobile application, where supported.
  • Your bank's official customer support email.
  • Visiting your nearest bank branch.
  • Contacting the lender directly.
  • The AutoPay or Mandates section of your UPI application.
Always Obtain Written Confirmation

Request written confirmation of cancellation and retain the acknowledgement or service request number for future reference.

Do not assume that submitting a cancellation request means the mandate has already been cancelled. Verify its final status with your bank or payment service provider.

3. Protect Yourself from Repeated Bounce Charges

When multiple lenders attempt automatic debits against an account with insufficient funds, repeated failed transactions may attract bank charges and contractual lender charges.

Reviewing and cancelling relevant mandates may help prevent future automatic debit attempts under those mandates once cancellation becomes effective.

However, cancelling a mandate does not reverse previously incurred charges or prevent all late-payment penalties. Discuss alternative repayment arrangements with your lenders.

4. Maintain a Record of All Mandates

Prepare a simple tracker containing the following information:

Information Purpose
Lender Name Identify the lender.
Bank Account Identify the linked bank account. Use masked account details for your tracker.
Mandate Reference / UMRN Track the unique mandate reference number.
Mandate Amount Understand the authorised debit limit.
Cancellation Request Date Track when your cancellation request was submitted.
Service Request Number Maintain supporting evidence for future follow-ups.
Current Status Active, pending cancellation, or cancelled.

Follow up with your bank until you receive confirmation that the mandate has been cancelled.

Keep all cancellation confirmations, email communications, and service request numbers securely stored for future reference.

5. Important Things to Remember

Cancelling a Mandate Does Not Cancel Your Loan

Cancelling an eNACH mandate is not the same as cancelling your loan. Your outstanding repayment obligations remain.

Missed payments may attract applicable charges, affect your credit score, and result in lawful recovery action.

Continue communicating with your lenders through official channels and request affordable EMI arrangements, restructuring, or settlement options based on your financial circumstances.

Do not accidentally cancel essential mandates related to insurance, utilities, or other payments you intend to maintain.

Regain Control of Your Finances

When you are already struggling with debt, watching your bank account accumulate additional charges can be emotionally exhausting. You cannot change the financial decisions you made in the past, but you can make more informed decisions today.

Prioritise essential living expenses, understand your financial obligations, and work towards a realistic repayment plan rather than repeatedly borrowing money to cover automatic deductions.

Taking control of your bank account does not mean abandoning your financial responsibilities. It means managing them with awareness, discipline, and a realistic plan.
Review your mandates. Avoid unnecessary charges. Regain financial control.

Cancelling a payment mandate does not cancel your loan or existing repayment obligations.
