Inform Trusted Contacts & References

Protect your loved ones from recovery harassment, overcome the fear of embarrassment, and prevent your personal relationships from becoming a source of financial pressure.

Do Not Let Fear Control Your Financial Decisions

One of the biggest fears borrowers experience when facing financial difficulties is the possibility of recovery agents contacting their family members, friends, relatives, and colleagues.

Some recovery agents may use this fear as a pressure tactic, threatening to disclose your financial situation to people you know. Out of fear of embarrassment, many borrowers end up taking additional loans to repay existing ones, pushing themselves deeper into the debt trap.

Informing trusted individuals in advance can help reduce the emotional impact of such harassment and prevent recovery agents from using your personal relationships as a source of pressure.

Do not allow the fear of embarrassment to control your financial decisions.

1. Identify People Who May Receive Recovery Calls

Prepare a private list of individuals whose contact details may already be available to lending companies or recovery agencies.

These may include:

  • References provided during loan applications.
  • Parents, siblings, and other family members.
  • Close friends and relatives.
  • Current and former colleagues.
  • Individuals whose mobile numbers appear in your UPI transactions.
  • Contacts previously accessible to loan applications.
  • Individuals whose information is publicly available through your social media profiles.

Review your previously shared bank statements to identify potentially exposed contacts.

For detailed guidance, refer to: Review Your Bank Statements

2. Inform Your Contacts Through a WhatsApp Status

If you are uncomfortable discussing your financial situation individually with friends, relatives, or colleagues, consider posting a general privacy alert on your WhatsApp status.

This can help alert your contacts about potential unwanted calls, suspected personal data misuse, and suspicious messages without disclosing your financial circumstances.

Sample WhatsApp Status – Privacy Alert

⚠️ Important Privacy Alert I suspect that some of my personal information and contact details may have been accessed or misused by unauthorised individuals. If you receive any suspicious calls, WhatsApp messages, or emails asking about me or sharing my personal information, please do not engage or disclose any details. Kindly block and report such numbers. If possible, share a screenshot with me for investigation and reporting purposes. Thank you for your cooperation.

Short WhatsApp Status

⚠️ Privacy Alert Suspected misuse of my personal data has come to my attention. If you receive any suspicious calls or messages regarding me, please ignore, block, and report them. Do not share any personal information with unknown callers.

You may also temporarily restrict your WhatsApp status visibility to trusted contacts.

Control Who Can View Your Status

Open WhatsApp and navigate to Settings → Privacy → Status.

Select Only share with to choose the trusted contacts you want to inform.

Menu names may vary depending on your device and application version. Review your settings before posting.

Posting a general privacy alert can help prepare your contacts for unwanted communications, reduce misunderstandings, and minimise the emotional pressure caused by recovery threats.

Keep Your Privacy Alert Accurate

Avoid claiming that your phone or accounts have been hacked unless this has actually been established.

If you genuinely suspect misuse of personal information, describe it as suspected personal data misuse. Adapt the sample messages to reflect your actual circumstances.

Your financial difficulties are private. You have the right to decide how much information you share and with whom.

3. Inform the References You Provided During Loan Applications

If you provided someone as a reference while applying for a loan, consider informing them before recovery agents attempt to contact them.

A reference is not automatically a guarantor or co-borrower. Merely providing someone's name and mobile number as a reference does not make that individual responsible for repaying your loan.

Explain that they are not required to negotiate repayment on your behalf or provide additional personal information to recovery agents.

If they experience repeated calls, threats, abusive language, or intimidation, encourage them to report the harassment directly.

A Reference Is Not Automatically a Guarantor

Unless an individual has separately accepted a legally binding repayment obligation, being named as a loan reference does not ordinarily make them responsible for your debt.

4. Guide Your Contacts on How to Handle Recovery Calls

Ask your trusted contacts to follow these precautions if they receive unwanted recovery calls.

Situation Recommended Action
Caller asks about your whereabouts. Avoid sharing personal information.
Caller demands repayment. Explain that they are not responsible for the loan unless legally obligated.
Caller uses abusive language. End the conversation and preserve available evidence.
Caller asks for additional contact numbers. Do not disclose anyone else's personal information.
Caller sends threatening WhatsApp messages. Capture screenshots before blocking or reporting.
Caller repeatedly contacts them. Enable call protection and report persistent harassment.
Caller threatens physical harm. Contact the police immediately.

Help your family and friends configure their phone privacy settings, enable spam protection, and restrict unknown callers where appropriate.

5. Encourage Affected Individuals to Report Harassment

If your friends, family members, or references receive abusive or threatening calls, they can also raise complaints about the harassment they have personally experienced.

Ask them to preserve available evidence, including:

  • Incoming call logs.
  • Mobile numbers used by recovery agents.
  • WhatsApp messages and screenshots.
  • Threatening or abusive communications.
  • Name of the recovery agency, if disclosed.
  • Name of the lender, if disclosed.
  • Dates and times of the incidents.

Encourage them to use appropriate complaint channels.

National Cybercrime Reporting Portal cybercrime.gov.in
Sanchar Saathi – Chakshu sancharsaathi.gov.in
TRAI – Unsolicited Commercial Communications 1909
RBI Complaint Management System cms.rbi.org.in
Emergency Police Assistance 112

The appropriate complaint channel depends on the nature of the harassment and applicable complaint eligibility requirements.

Use the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal for suspected cyber offences, Chakshu for eligible suspected fraud communications, and 1909 for eligible unsolicited commercial communications.

For complaints against regulated lenders, first approach the concerned lender's grievance department. Eligible unresolved complaints may subsequently be escalated through the RBI Complaint Management System.

Do Not Ignore Serious Threats

If you or your contacts receive credible threats of physical harm, contact the police immediately. Do not wait for a lender's grievance investigation.

6. Stop Allowing Fear to Drive Your Financial Decisions

Some borrowers continue taking new loans simply because they are afraid that their existing lenders will contact their family members or friends.

But how long can you continue borrowing money to protect yourself from embarrassment?

Every additional loan may increase your financial burden, extend your repayment difficulties, and create another opportunity for recovery pressure.

Informing trusted individuals in advance can reduce the power of threats and allow you to focus on resolving your financial situation rather than constantly worrying about who might receive the next recovery call.

Your priority should be breaking the debt cycle and developing a sustainable repayment plan.

For additional guidance, refer to: Break the Re-loan Cycle

You Do Not Have to Fight This Battle Alone

Financial difficulties can happen to anyone. A temporary financial crisis does not define your character, your abilities, or your future.

You may feel embarrassed about discussing your situation, but continuing to suffer in silence can make the experience even more difficult.

Choose carefully whom you trust. You do not owe everyone an explanation, and you have the right to maintain your privacy.

The people who genuinely care about you may be more understanding than you expect.

Your debt is a financial challenge, not a measure of your worth as a human being.

Do not sacrifice your financial future simply to hide your present difficulties. Protect your relationships, seek support, hold recovery agencies accountable for misconduct, and focus on rebuilding your life.

Break the silence. Overcome the fear. Rebuild your life.
