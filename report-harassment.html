Report Harassment & Raise a Complaint

Protect your dignity, preserve your evidence, report inappropriate recovery practices, and seek accountability through lawful grievance redressal mechanisms.

Taking a loan does not mean surrendering your right to privacy, dignity and respectful treatment. While lending institutions have the right to recover outstanding dues through lawful means, recovery agents cannot use your financial difficulties as an excuse to threaten, abuse, intimidate or publicly humiliate you.

Many borrowers remain silent because they fear embarrassment, damage to their professional reputation or harassment of their family members. This fear often pushes them into taking additional loans, making their financial situation even worse.

Instead of borrowing again under pressure, use the available grievance redressal mechanisms to report inappropriate recovery practices and demand accountability.

Do not allow fear to push you further into debt.

Collect and Preserve Evidence

Maintaining proper evidence is one of the most important aspects of raising a complaint against a lending institution or recovery agency.

Before deleting messages, blocking phone numbers or uninstalling loan applications, preserve all available evidence.

Type of Harassment Evidence to Preserve
Abusive recovery calls Phone numbers, call logs and available lawful recordings
WhatsApp harassment Screenshots and chat history
Contacting family or friends Screenshots and available call details
Workplace harassment Emails, message headers and screenshots
Threatening home visits Messages, recordings and available CCTV footage
OTP bombing Screenshots with dates and timestamps
Suspected data misuse Evidence of information accessed or disclosed
Misleading legal threats Notices, emails and supporting documents
Social media harassment Screenshots, profile links and messages

Create a separate folder for each lending institution and maintain your evidence in chronological order.

Do not be discouraged if you do not have call recordings or complete evidence. Report the incident using whatever genuine information is available and request an investigation.

Preserve Original Evidence

Do not fabricate evidence or modify screenshots in a misleading manner. Keep original messages, email files and supporting documents securely. Share sensitive information only with appropriate authorities through verified channels.

Contact the Grievance and Nodal Officers

Send a formal email to the Grievance Redressal Officer and Nodal Officer of the lending institution.

Many digital lending applications operate on behalf of banks or Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs). Therefore, it is important to identify the actual lending institution rather than communicating only with the loan application or its collection executives.

You can find the lender's details in your loan agreement, sanction letter, Key Fact Statement or official website.

Your complaint should include:

  • Loan account number and lending application name.
  • Details of harassment, including dates and times.
  • Phone numbers and email addresses used by recovery agents.
  • Details of inappropriate third-party contact.
  • Available screenshots, call logs and supporting evidence.
  • Details of suspected personal data misuse.
  • Previous complaints and communications.
  • Corrective action requested from the lending institution.

Request a Proper Investigation

Request the lending institution to investigate the conduct of its recovery agents, explain how personal contact information was obtained, stop inappropriate recovery communications and provide a written investigation response.

If the harassment continues, update the existing grievance email thread with additional evidence. Ask the grievance department to coordinate internally with its collection and recovery teams.

Preserve all written communications, including evidence of the date on which the lender received your complaint, as this will be important when escalating your complaint to RBI.

Do not rely solely on verbal assurances. Always request written confirmation.

Raise a CPGRAMS Complaint

🏛

Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS)

Government of India

https://pgportal.gov.in/
Raise a CPGRAMS Complaint →

CPGRAMS is an official Government of India platform that allows citizens to raise grievances with concerned government ministries and departments.

If your complaint involves suspected regulatory violations, inappropriate recovery practices or other applicable supervisory concerns regarding a lending institution, you may also raise a grievance through CPGRAMS.

Register or sign in to the portal and select the option to lodge a public grievance. Select the Ministry of Finance, Department of Financial Services or relevant authority, depending on the organisation and complaint category available.

Clearly mention the name of the lending application, its associated bank or NBFC, details of harassment and the suspected regulatory violations.

Attach your grievance email and relevant supporting evidence. Once the complaint is submitted, save the registration number and regularly monitor its status.

What If Your CPGRAMS Complaint Is Closed Without Resolution?

A complaint marked as closed does not necessarily mean your concerns have been satisfactorily addressed.

Review the response carefully. If the authority or lending institution has failed to address your specific concerns, submit appropriate feedback and use the appeal facility wherever available.

Explain clearly which parts of your complaint remain unresolved and what further investigation or corrective action you are requesting.

Important

CPGRAMS is an additional government grievance mechanism. It does not replace the RBI Ombudsman process and may forward grievances to the appropriate authority.

Register a Cybercrime Complaint

🛡

National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal

Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India

https://cybercrime.gov.in/
Raise a Cybercrime Complaint →

If recovery agents are involved in suspected unlawful access to personal information, digital harassment, online threats, blackmail or other suspected cyber offences, register a complaint through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.

Examples of incidents that may warrant cybercrime reporting include:

  • Suspected unauthorised extraction of mobile contacts.
  • Suspected misuse of information obtained through bank statements.
  • Threatening or abusive WhatsApp messages.
  • Creation of WhatsApp groups to publicly humiliate borrowers.
  • Circulation of personal or manipulated photographs.
  • Repeated OTP bombing.
  • Online blackmail and extortion.
  • Impersonation of police officers or government officials.
  • Fraudulent payment links.
  • Suspected unauthorised disclosure of confidential information.

Register or sign in to the cybercrime portal, select the appropriate complaint category and provide a factual description of the incident.

Include available phone numbers, WhatsApp numbers, email addresses, screenshots, transaction details and other relevant evidence.

Save the acknowledgement number after submitting the complaint.

What If You Do Not Know Which Loan Application Is Responsible?

Recovery agents may use unidentified phone numbers, newly created email accounts or personal WhatsApp accounts without disclosing the lending institution they represent.

You can still report the incident.

Provide the available evidence and request the authorities to investigate the identity of the individuals involved. Clearly distinguish confirmed information from suspected involvement.

Do not make unsupported allegations against a specific lending institution.

Involve Your Local Police and Cyber Cell

Do not restrict yourself to online complaints when you are experiencing serious threats, persistent intimidation or suspected criminal conduct.

You have the option to visit your local police station or cybercrime police station and submit a written complaint.

Consider involving the police when:

  • Recovery agents threaten physical harm.
  • Individuals intimidate or threaten your family members.
  • Recovery agents threaten unlawful workplace visits.
  • Someone attempts to blackmail you using personal information.
  • Individuals impersonate police officials.
  • Recovery agents create disturbances at your residence.
  • You receive threatening communications from unidentified numbers.
  • There is suspected criminal misuse of personal information.

Where the facts disclose a cognizable offence, request appropriate action under applicable law, including registration of an FIR.

You do not need to complete the CPGRAMS or lender grievance process before seeking police assistance.

What If Your Local Police Station Does Not Help?

If your complaint is not appropriately addressed, escalate the matter in writing to the relevant senior police authorities.

Depending on the police administration in your area, you may approach:

  • Station House Officer (SHO).
  • Assistant Commissioner of Police (ACP).
  • Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP).
  • Superintendent of Police (SP).
  • Commissioner of Police.
  • State Cybercrime Nodal Officer.

Where registration of a cognizable offence is refused, seek appropriate legal assistance regarding further remedies available under law.

Maintain a record of every written complaint, acknowledgement and response.

Your Safety Comes First

Never wait for a lender's investigation if your physical safety or the safety of your family is at risk.

Contact emergency police assistance immediately if you are in danger.

Escalate Unresolved Complaints to RBI

🏛

RBI Complaint Management System (CMS)

Reserve Bank of India

https://cms.rbi.org.in/
Raise an RBI Complaint →

If the lending institution's Grievance Officer or Nodal Officer fails to resolve your complaint satisfactorily, you may escalate the matter through the Reserve Bank of India's Complaint Management System.

The RBI Ombudsman mechanism provides eligible customers with a grievance redressal platform without charging a complaint filing fee.

When Can You Escalate Your Complaint?

Grievance Status Recommended Action
No response within 30 days Escalate to RBI, subject to applicable timelines and eligibility.
Complaint rejected Escalate earlier, when eligible.
Unsatisfactory investigation response Escalate earlier, when eligible.
Harassment continues despite assurances Preserve additional evidence and escalate when eligible.
Generic responses without addressing concerns Document unresolved issues and escalate when eligible.
Complaint satisfactorily resolved Preserve the resolution documents.
Understanding the 30-Day Period

The standard 30-day period is calculated from the date the lending institution receives your formal grievance, not from the date of your CPGRAMS or cybercrime complaint.

You do not necessarily need to wait 30 days if the lending institution has already rejected your complaint or provided an unsatisfactory response, subject to the applicable scheme.

What Should You Include in Your RBI Complaint?

When submitting your RBI complaint, provide the name of the actual regulated lending institution, your original grievance submission date, details of harassment and the specific concerns that remain unresolved.

Attach your original grievance email, responses received from the lender, relevant screenshots, available recordings and other supporting evidence.

You may also include your CPGRAMS registration number, cybercrime acknowledgement number and local police complaint reference wherever relevant.

Explain why the lender's response is unsatisfactory and clearly specify the corrective action you are requesting.

Depending on the circumstances and applicable regulations, you may request an investigation into:

  • Inappropriate recovery practices.
  • Failure to adequately investigate harassment complaints.
  • Suspected misuse of personal information.
  • Inappropriate disclosure of financial information to third parties.
  • Deficiencies in grievance redressal.
  • Disputed interest and penal charges.
  • Inaccurate credit bureau reporting.
  • Applicable compensation for harassment or consequential losses.

Compensation and other remedies are subject to eligibility, evidence and the concerned authority's decision.

Check the current RBI Ombudsman Scheme for applicable filing deadlines, eligibility conditions and restrictions on complaints involving matters already pending before or decided by other forums. Disclose relevant existing proceedings when required.

Where applicable, complaints involving regulated entities outside the Ombudsman Scheme may be handled through RBI's Consumer Education and Protection Cells (CEPCs).

Maintain Your Complaint Records

If you are dealing with multiple lending institutions, maintain a personal record of your complaints using an Excel spreadsheet or a secure folder on your own device.

Keep your grievance emails, CPGRAMS registration numbers, cybercrime acknowledgements, police complaint references and RBI complaint numbers organised. Regularly review their status and follow up on unresolved concerns.

Keep all supporting documents in separate lender-specific folders. Continue updating existing complaints with new evidence rather than unnecessarily creating duplicate complaints about the same incident.

For privacy and security reasons, do not share confidential loan information or complaint documents on public platforms.

Understand Your Rights Under RBI Guidelines

The Reserve Bank of India has issued regulatory directions governing the conduct of lending institutions and recovery agents.

RBI Recovery Agent Guidelines

Circular Date: 12 August 2022

Circular Number: RBI/2022-23/108

Reference: DOR.ORG.REC.65/21.04.158/2022-23

Subject: Outsourcing of Financial Services – Responsibilities of regulated entities employing Recovery Agents.

Read RBI Circular →

Applicable recovery guidelines prohibit practices such as intimidation, harassment, public humiliation, inappropriate intrusion into personal privacy, threatening communications and persistent recovery calls.

The regulated lending institution remains responsible for the conduct of its outsourced recovery agents under applicable regulations.

Consult the latest RBI directions applicable to your lender and loan category, as regulatory requirements may be updated or consolidated into subsequent directions.

Harassment Complaints Do Not Cancel Your Loan

Raising a harassment complaint does not automatically cancel outstanding loans, waive interest or penalties, prevent legitimate credit reporting or suspend lawful recovery proceedings.

Continue working towards a sustainable financial resolution while pursuing your complaints.

Your Silence Should Not Become Someone Else's Power

Recovery agents may attempt to exploit your financial difficulties, fear of embarrassment and concern for your family to pressure you into making immediate payments.

But you do not have to remain silent.

Do not allow the fear of recovery calls, workplace harassment or public humiliation to destroy your financial future.

Report genuine misconduct. Preserve your evidence. Involve the appropriate authorities. Follow up until your concerns have been properly addressed.

Do not respond to harassment with abuse, threats or unlawful retaliation. Let your evidence and formal complaints speak for you.

Even if your complaint does not produce immediate results, do not lose hope. Continue pursuing appropriate lawful remedies while focusing on rebuilding your financial life.

Every genuine complaint may help bring inappropriate recovery practices to the attention of the authorities and protect other borrowers facing similar circumstances.

You may be going through financial difficulties today, but those difficulties do not define your character, your worth or your future.

Being a borrower does not make you a criminal. Your debt does not take away your dignity.
Break the silence. Raise your voice. Know your rights. Demand accountability. Preserve your dignity.
